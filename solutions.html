Solutions

Five industries, one method.

The loop is the same everywhere. What changes is the specification: what counts as good, what counts as disqualifying, and which constraint you are not allowed to trade away.

Energy storage

Battery materials

Electrolytes, additives, and binders — from molecular property to cell-level performance.

  • Oxidative stability and transport screened in silico before formulation
  • Formulations proposed against voltage, temperature, and cost limits together
  • Cycling data from the pilot line fed straight back into the ranking model

Specialty chemicals

Fine chemicals

Route design and condition optimization with yield, purity, and cost inside the objective.

  • Retrosynthetic routes ranked by cost of goods
  • Condition search that respects your existing reactor fleet
  • Impurity profiles predicted before the first scale-up run

Pharmaceuticals

Pharmaceutical intermediates

Process chemistry with impurity and scale-up risk modeled from the first candidate.

  • Genotoxic impurity risk flagged at route-selection time
  • Crystallization and polymorph screening planned by the agent
  • Every decision carries a citation trail for the regulatory file

Semiconductor

Electronic chemicals

Photoresists, CMP slurries, and packaging materials held to semiconductor purity.

  • Formulation search under ppb-level contamination constraints
  • Optical and etch behaviour predicted across process windows
  • Supplier lot variation modeled as an explicit variable

Thermal management

Thermal materials

Interface materials and coolants selected against real device duty cycles.

  • Conductivity, viscosity, and aging traded off in one objective
  • Device-level thermal simulation closes the loop on formulation
  • Reliability tested against the duty cycle you actually ship

Not on this list?

If your problem can be written as a specification and measured in a lab, the method applies. Tell us the specification.
