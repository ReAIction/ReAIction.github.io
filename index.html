The bottleneck

The bottleneck was never the shortlist.

Models produce candidates faster than any lab can test them. What decides which hundred get made is what the plant can actually run — the equipment on the floor, the cost per kilo, the purity the customer will accept.

Choosing wrong is expensive late. A new material takes about ten years to reach a qualified production process, and two to twenty million dollars to carry one formulation that far — and most of that is spent after the shortlist, not before it.

The agent

Kelvin runs the loop.

One agent, addressed in plain language, across the whole cycle. Ask it for a higher-voltage electrolyte and it plans the campaign — not just the calculation.

How the platform works

How it is constrained

Optimised for the plant you already have.

The constraints that decide whether a candidate can be made are in the objective function, not in a filter applied afterwards.

Cost of goods, not step count
Retrosynthetic routes are ranked by what the material costs to make at scale, not by how few steps it takes.
Your reactor fleet, not an ideal one
Condition search is constrained to the equipment you already run.
Lot variation as a variable
Supplier batch-to-batch drift is modelled explicitly, not discovered at scale-up.
The pilot line closes the loop
Cycling and process data from the pilot line re-rank the model directly.
